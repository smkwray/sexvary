Sex Differences in Variability Across Public-Use Datasets

This project estimates where sex differences in score variability appear—and where they do not—across cognitive, achievement, and physical traits, using 14 public-use datasets spanning school-age children through older adults. The analysis is descriptive, not causal.

Headline Findings

The strongest confirmatory evidence currently spans 50 headline-eligible cells across 14 live datasets. Within that headline layer, the prevailing pattern is male-greater variability, but the pattern is not universal.

The strongest positive confirmatory cell is Numeracy in PIAAC cycle 2, adults aged 60–65 (variance ratio 1.46×—males 46% more variable). The supporting-evidence layer adds 172 inferential rows from NHANES, HRS, and PSID that broaden age and domain coverage but remain below headline quality.

Strongest counterexample

Reading achievement in ECLS-K:2011, fall kindergarten 2010, shows substantially greater female variability (variance ratio 0.10×—male variance is roughly one-tenth of female). This is the most extreme cell in the entire headline layer and should temper any interpretation that male-greater variability is universal across all traits, ages, and instruments.

Confirmatory Forest Plot

Each dot is one headline-eligible cell. Values above 1.0× indicate male-greater variability; below 1.0× indicate female-greater.

Median Variance Ratio by Dataset

Bars show the median variance ratio within each dataset–trait family combination. Longer bars to the right of 1.0× indicate larger median male-greater variability.
